1
Review in the top bar (✎ ✓), in the same place as the other panes. It replaces the inspector in the right column; closing it brings the inspector back. No mode picker.
2
Hovering draws the element a click would pin to, in the selection blue with a small hint. Pins are amber, so selecting and remarking never look alike.
3
A click leaves a remark: a numbered pin on the element, a card in the tray, its text box focused. Dragging, resizing and retyping are off while the pane is open; nothing is selected.
4
The card says where it is in concrete terms: handle, tag, the element’s own words, the artboard. Clicking a card scrolls to its element (the focused card is outlined in blue and so is its pin).
5
A second click on a pinned element goes back to its remark. A click on the empty artboard remarks on the whole artboard.
6
One button, always at the bottom: Send N remarks to Claude Code. Empty remarks stay behind. × removes one with a toast Undo; Clear removes all with the same Undo.
7
Remarks live in this browser until sent (a reload keeps them); they are not in the project files.
Open
• “Remark”, not “note”: canvas has sticky notes and slides have speaker notes (one word, one meaning).
• One remark per element. A second click goes back to the first.
• Claude Code only: a batch spans files, the fast AI edits one.
• A sent batch is not kept in the tray; the conversation holds it. A “last batch, put back” is not drawn.
• A remark on a region (drag a rectangle) is not designed.
• Docs: “send all open threads” would be the same idea over comments.json.
