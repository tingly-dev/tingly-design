# Motion

Choose "动效" (motion) when creating a project, then pick its size: Landscape 16:9 (YouTube, Bilibili, X/Twitter, PPT), Vertical 9:16 (Douyin, WeChat Channels, TikTok, Reels), Square, Portrait 4:5 or 3:4 (Instagram, Xiaohongshu), Classic 4:3 (older PPT) or any width and height. The next piece starts in the size you used last, and your own sizes are kept next to the presets. The sample [`?p=design-motion`](http://localhost:5173/?p=design-motion) is a 36 s promo for Tingly Design in 16:9 and 9:16 from the same scenes, with its own cover.

![Motion editor: the cover on the stage, the timeline and scene strip below, formats and speed in the top bar](../../images/motion.png)

```
projects/<name>/
  motion.json          title, formats and their sizes, fps, speed, scene order, cover
  scenes/<id>.html     one file per scene: exactly one <section id="<id>">, the same closed subset as a slide
  renders/             exported videos (not tracked)
```

- **Stage and player**: while paused, the stage shows the scene under the playhead at that moment and you edit it like a slide. Space plays: the player takes over and runs the whole timeline, transitions included; pause and the stage comes back where it stopped. ← / → step one frame, with Shift one second. The ruler and the scene strip below the stage share one time axis: each scene's block starts where the scene starts and runs until the next one takes over, and the playhead runs through both. Click or drag along the ruler to scrub; click a scene to jump to its start, double-click to jump to its end. To change a scene's length, drag the right edge of its block (0.1 s steps, Esc cancels), type it in the length field above the timeline, or use the panel. On a scene's first moment the stage draws every element where it lands, so the scene reads as a page to edit (the label under the timeline says 落定); step one frame on to see the real frame. The cover is optional and sits before 0 s, in its own slot at the left of the timeline: click it to edit it (it is what a thumbnail of the video shows, and the cover picture of the exported file), click “+ Cover” to add one, or × to go back to using the first frame. It opens on the cover when there is one. The cover can also be one frame of the video instead of a page: pause on the frame you want and press “Use as cover” above the timeline. It stands on the ruler as a pin; drag the pin (or press ← / → on it) to move it, and the piece opens on that frame. Its time is written at reading pace, like every time in the piece, so changing the speed moves it with the scenes.
- **Editing on the stage**: click to select, double-click to retype, drag a handle to resize (Shift keeps the ratio, Alt resizes from the centre, edges snap to other elements). The panel on the right has the element's styles, the scene's length, transition and background when the scene is selected, and the piece's sizes when nothing is. Elements not on screen at the playhead can't be clicked; pick them from the layers, which say when they come in and have the same hide and lock as the canvas (hidden elements are left out of the player and the render too). A selected element is shown where it lands, not half way through its entrance. Undo works as everywhere else.
- **Sizes**: every size in `motion.json` plays from the same scenes; switch between them in the top bar, and add, resize or remove them in the panel (横版 16:9, 竖版 9:16, 方形, 竖图 4:5 or your own). Edits made while looking at the first size change every size; in another size they are written as that size's override (`data-tall="…"`), and the panel says which. Click an override chip to go back to the shared value.
- **Notes**: the Notes button in the top bar opens a note on the whole piece (`note` in `motion.json`) and one on the scene on the stage (an `<aside>` at the end of its file, the same as a slide's speaker notes). Write who it is for, the tone, what a scene should say; they are never drawn or rendered, and Claude reads them with your requests. A dot marks the scenes that have one.
- **Importing pictures**: drop, paste or use the import button to put pictures and SVGs on the scene under the playhead. They fade in from the playhead (from the start on a scene's landed first moment) and are kept in `uploads/`.
- **Built-in effects**: an empty embed with `data-fx` (`<x-embed data-fx="aurora" data-seek …>`) draws one of sixteen WebGL effects: backgrounds (`flow`, `aurora`, `mesh`, `plasma`, `waves`, `grid`, `stars`, `halftone`) and layers (`edge-glow` around a card, `sweep` across a title, `rays`, `ripple`, `particles`, `light-leak`, `scanlines`, `grain`), each with its values written after it (`colors: #3d7bff #9861ff; speed: 0.5`). `scale`, `direction`, `at` and `region` reshape them: `aurora; region: 0% 55% 100% 45%` is an aurora along the bottom only. They draw the same frame in the editor, the player and the render. Where WebGL2 is off they draw a simpler CSS version and the editor says so; `tingly-design check` reports a name or value that does not read.
- **Importing from a project**: the Import / Export menu's "From a project…" lists the artboards of your canvases. The one you pick is copied into this piece (`uploads/<canvas>-<board>/`) and goes onto the scene under the playhead. An artboard that moves with time (an effect) fills the frame behind everything and is drawn frame by frame from the scene's time, on the stage, in the player and in the render; a still one fades in at the playhead. Select it to set its options, open the original, or update it from its canvas.
- **Speed**: timing is written at reading pace and `speed` in `motion.json` sets how fast the video plays. The speed menu tries another pace without touching any file.
- **Check**: lists timing problems such as an exit before the entry, text on screen for less than 0.8 s, or an embed that cannot be exported.
- **AI**: the box at the bottom works on the scene under the playhead, or the selected element. Pick the fast AI for a small change, which lands on the stage at once, or Claude Code for a larger one; Claude Code is told which scene, moment and size you are looking at.
- **Save frame**: “Save frame as image” saves the picture at the playhead, at the size you are looking at, as a PNG.
- **Export**: “Import / Export” → “Video…” opens a dialog with one summary line (“MP4 · 1920×1080 · 30 fps · whole piece”) and an Export button; that is all most exports need. “More options” folds out the format (MP4, with WebM where the browser cannot write H.264; WebM; GIF), the size (the piece's own, or a smaller common height), the frame rate, the quality and the range (the whole piece, or just the scene under the playhead, which keeps its share of the sound). The format, size, frame rate and quality of the last video come back as the starting point next time (a GIF does not change them). Every video file carries an audio track; a piece with no sound gets a silent one, because some platforms refuse a file without. “GIF…” in the same menu opens the dialog with GIF already chosen; a GIF defaults to a small size and 15 fps. It is made in this tab, with nothing to install: MP4 where the browser can write H.264 and AAC (Chrome, Edge, Safari), otherwise WebM, and the message says which and why. Keep the tab in front until it finishes; a progress button with a cancel appears in the bar. Fonts and audio files come along. A piece with an embed (`<x-embed>`) or a video layer is drawn by the Chrome or Edge on the machine running the server instead (it needs ffmpeg too), and the dialog says so; that route has the same size, frame rate and range options, but the format is MP4 or GIF (no WebM). When the machine has neither, the video is still made in the tab: each embed hands over a picture of itself for every frame (a little slower); a scene whose embed could not is named in the message, and a video layer is not frame-exact there. The file carries the cover as its cover picture, which file browsers and players show as the thumbnail (MP4 and WebM, as from the command line). The dialog's “More options” also has a box to put the cover in the video's first frame, for apps that only show a video's first frame; it is off unless `"coverFirstFrame": true` is in `motion.json`; “Cover picture” in the same menu saves the cover as a PNG, in the format on screen (with no cover, the first frame); “PNG image” saves the frame at the playhead and “Copy as PNG” puts it on the clipboard. From the [command line](../cli.md), `tingly-design export <name> --as mp4 [--format wide]` writes an MP4 per format with the cover as its cover art, plus the cover PNG, to `renders/`; `--as gif` and `--as png` (the cover alone) work the same way. That uses the same Chrome or Edge and ffmpeg. `--as html` writes a single-file HTML player anyone can open (every format, a scrubber, the speeds), with nothing to install; `--scale 2` draws each frame at twice the size and brings it back down, for sharper type. At the end of the same menu, “Whole project (.zip)” saves the project itself: every file and its change log; “Project files only (.zip)” leaves the change log out. “Import a project package (.zip)…” in the home page's New menu opens it again (a name already taken gets `-2`), and so does a project folder you zipped yourself.

Timing lives on the elements: `data-duration` and `data-transition` on the `<section>`, `data-in` / `data-out` presets (`rise`, `drop`, `type`, `count`…) and `data-keys` keyframe tracks on anything inside.
